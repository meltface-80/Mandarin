Your account

One account, kept on the server itself — nothing online — or, if you'd rather, none.

  1. Open http://<server-ip>:3500 on a device on your home network (a browser, the iPhone home-screen app, or the Android app) and choose a username and password, or tap Use without an account.
  2. With an account, every other device signs in with it once and is remembered.
  3. Settings → Account lists every device, with a Sign out button for each, and changes the password.

Ask for a sign-in

Settings → Account → Ask for a sign-in (on the page and in the Android app) switches the sign-in on or off. An install that had an account before v1.1.8 keeps asking until you switch it off. Off, anything on your home network or on your own Tailscale network comes straight in (with the built-in Tailscale, only your own devices on it: not one shared in from someone else's tailnet, nor an address your internet provider gives from the same range): a TV showing the shelf or the wall needs nothing typed. Each browser and app is still a device of its own, listed in Settings → Account. From anywhere else (a port forwarded to the internet) a sign-in is still needed, and with no account there is none to give. Switching it on with no account asks you to create the account first; devices already in stay in until you sign them out.

With the sign-in off, a device comes in without one only when it asks for the server by a name used at home — its address, a bare name, or one ending .local, .lan, .home.arpa or .ts.net — so another web site can't reach it through a name of its own. Anything but a read must come from Mandarin's own page or the Android app: a page of another site open in a browser at home can't restart the server, change a setting or restore a backup. A device let in without a password works only from home and your tailnet, never from the internet.

Behind a reverse proxy (an nginx, Caddy or tailscale serve on the same machine, or one elsewhere on your network), name it in TRUSTED_PROXIES (TRUSTED_PROXIES=127.0.0.1 for one on the same machine) and have it say who asked (X-Forwarded-For, X-Real-IP or Forwarded). Mandarin believes only the proxies named and its own built-in Tailscale: a request that names a caller through anything else, or a named proxy that names nobody, is taken as the internet, never home, and the log says so.

Your password stays yours

The password never crosses the network, even on plain http: devices prove they know it with SRP (the scheme Apple uses for HomeKit pairing), and the server proves it knows the account back. The server stores only an SRP verifier, never the password.

Five wrong passwords from one address in 15 minutes lock it out for 15 minutes (for IPv6, its whole /64), and guesses sent all at once count as they arrive, so no more than five are ever tried.

Sonos speakers can't sign in: the addresses they're given carry a signature, and the speakers' own addresses are let through, so playback is unaffected.

Forgot the password?

On Mandarin OS, type passwordreset at its own screen (a keyboard plugged in), then yes. Installed on Linux, run on the server:

sudo -u mandarin env DATA_DIR=/var/lib/mandarin /opt/mandarin/server/bin/mandarin-server reset-password

It removes the account and signs every device out; your library, edits and history stay. Then create the account again from a device at home.
