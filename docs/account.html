Your account

One account, kept on the server itself — nothing online — or, if you'd rather, none.

  1. Open http://<server-ip>:3500 on a device on your home network (a browser, the iPhone home-screen app, or the Android app) and choose a username and password, or tap Use without an account.
  2. With an account, every other device signs in with it once and is remembered.
  3. Settings → Account lists every device, with a Sign out button for each, and changes the password.

Ask for a sign-in

Settings → Account → Ask for a sign-in switches the sign-in on or off. Off, anything on your home network or on your own Tailscale network comes straight in: a TV showing the shelf or the wall needs nothing typed. From anywhere else (a port forwarded to the internet) a sign-in is still needed, and with no account there is none to give.

With the sign-in off, a device comes in without one only when it asks for the server by a name used at home — its address, a bare name, or one ending .local, .lan, .home.arpa or .ts.net. Behind a reverse proxy on the same machine, the proxy must say who asked (X-Forwarded-For, X-Real-IP or Forwarded).

Your password stays yours

The password never crosses the network, even on plain http: devices prove they know it with SRP (the scheme Apple uses for HomeKit pairing), and the server stores only a verifier, never the password. Five wrong passwords from one address in 15 minutes lock it out for 15 minutes.

Forgot the password?

On Mandarin OS, type passwordreset at its own screen (a keyboard plugged in), then yes. Installed on Linux, run on the server:

sudo -u mandarin env DATA_DIR=/var/lib/mandarin /opt/mandarin/server/bin/mandarin-server reset-password

It removes the account and signs every device out; your library, edits and history stay. Then create the account again from a device at home.
