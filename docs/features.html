Features

Tap i on any feature for how to switch it on and use it. New marks what's new in v1.3.1 (v1.3.2 adds no new features).

🏠Home

Rows of albums under a greeting: Not played in 6 months, Listen later, Favourites, Recently played, Smart Picks, Label of the week, Random albums, Library and Browse by genre.

How to set it up and use it. Open ☰ → Home. Tap a row's title for all of it. To reorder rows or hide one, open Settings → Setup → Home Screen, hold a row's grip and drag it, or switch it off.

👆Phones and tablets

On a touchscreen: a bar at the foot on every screen, Now playing too (Menu, Home, Library, Artists, Shelf, Search), and the mini player docked on it on a tablet.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to set up: a phone or tablet gets it in the browser, the iPhone home-screen app and the Android app; a computer with a mouse keeps the top bar. Shelf is in the bar while it is switched on (Settings → Setup → Shelf). ☰, the bar's first button, opens the side menu from any screen. Tap Home or Library again to go back to the top; Search opens its field across the screen. A phone's mini player floats over the bar; a tablet's is docked on it, edge to edge. In the Android app a phone is held upright; a tablet, and a foldable opened, turns and is laid out as a tablet.

🎲Random Album

The first tile under the greeting chooses an album you haven't played in 12 months (until Mandarin has 12 months of history, any album) and offers it to play.

How to set it up and use it. Choose where to play with the speaker button, then tap Random Album. Its disc turns while an album is chosen; then tap Play now, Play next or Queue, or close it.

📅Album of the day

One album you haven't played in a year or more (or never), the same on every device, chosen by the server at 00:01 and kept all day.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to set up: it sits beside Random Album on Home, with its own switch in Settings → Setup → Home Screen. 00:01 is in the server's time zone.

⏳Not played in 6 months

Albums you haven't played in six months. The row stays hidden until Mandarin has six months of your listening.

How to set it up and use it. A Home row; tap its title for all of them. Move or hide it in Settings → Setup → Home Screen.

📚Library

Every album, sorted by name, artist, release date, date added, plays, last played or random, and narrowed by genre, decade, label, format, sample rate, bit depth or first letter.

How to set it up and use it. Tap the Library row's title on Home, or Library in a phone's or tablet's row. Sort and Focus are in the top bar (at the top right on a phone or tablet); Focus also has Added in the last and Listening (never played, not in 6 or 12 months). ☰ → Random albums opens a shuffled wall.

🗄️Shelf

A record shop's shelf: your albums' spines in a 3D row, narrowed by genre, artist, year, label, type, format or favourites, put in order, or spun to land anywhere.

How to set it up and use it. Its switch is Settings → Setup → Shelf (off on a new install); off, it is off entirely and not offered. Open ☰ → Shelf, Shelf in a phone's or tablet's row, http://<server-ip>:3500/shelf, or Shelf › on the wall display; the Android app draws it itself. Swipe for one album, swipe and hold to keep turning, flick to spin; tap a spine to bring it to the front, and the one in front to open the album: the library's own album page, in the Shelf's room, with its track times, the heart, Play now, Queue and ⋯; hold a track to choose tracks and play or queue those. The choices' eight tabs (Genres, Artists, Years, Labels, Types, Formats, Favourites, Order) narrow the shelf; #N, A → Z and Z → A put the genres in order. Order has A → Z by artist, by label and catalogue number, by catalogue number, by year, and random; each device keeps the one chosen. The bar at its foot has what's playing, Spin, the zone and the volume; the queue comes in from the right.

🔍Search

Albums, artists and labels as you type, and Qobuz's and Tidal's catalogues beside them: an album from either opens and plays whether or not it is in your library.

How to set it up and use it. Tap the magnifying glass in the top bar (in the row at the foot of a phone's or tablet's screen) and type. Labels show when Record labels is on. Signed in to Qobuz or Tidal (Settings → Services), the service's artists and albums follow the library's, from the Library wall's filter too; tap a cover for the album's page, an artist for their page.

💿Album pages

Tracks, year and label, write-ups from Wikipedia and Qobuz, Pitchfork's score, and every version of the album (an original and its remaster) as one album, the best its main.

How to set it up and use it. Tap any album. Play Now and Queue are on the page; ⋯ has Play Next, Shuffle, Radio, Listen later, Share card, Make this the main version, Edit album and (in the Android app) Download. On a phone, Tracks, Credits, About and Versions are tabs, held at the top as the page scrolls; Versions lists each version with its edition and rate, a tap showing it in place. Versions are told by MusicBrainz's release group, else one act and title with its version words off; a list shows the main one with 2 versions under it. On a computer, swipe sideways or press ← → for the previous or next album. The track playing has a small disc turning beside its number. A classical album is set out as its works and movements, its composers and performers in its head. On a computer, a TV or a tablet held sideways the page fills the screen in three columns, in the browser and in the Android app on a tablet: the cover, its credits and more by the artist on the left; About this album and about its artist in the middle, with Read more; the tracks on the right.

🧭More by, and similar

Under an album's review: the artist's other albums, the albums they appear on, and, with a Last.fm key, similar artists and similar albums from your own library.

How to set it up and use it. Open any album. For the Last.fm rows, get a free key at last.fm/api/account/create and paste it in Settings → Setup → API Keys (or set LASTFM_KEY). Only albums you have are shown, each opening here; to scrobble too, see Last.fm scrobbling.

🎤Artists

Every act as a round picture with their name and album count, sorted A to Z, by most albums or at random. Two acts of one name are two artists.

How to set it up and use it. On a phone or tablet, tap Artists in the row at the foot of the screen. Sort is at the top right; Search in the row finds an artist by part of the name. Tap one for their page. Two acts of the same name (Airbag from Norway, Airbag from Argentina) are told apart by the MusicBrainz artist ID in their files' tags (as Picard writes it), else by Mandarin's own MusicBrainz matches (Settings → Library Scanner), each with its own page, picture and a word to tell them apart.

🧑‍🎤Artist pages

In tabs, as an album's page: Overview (their albums, similar artists, similar albums you have), Discography and About (biography, members or bands). One artist however a sleeve credits them.

How to set it up and use it. Tap an artist's name on an album's page, on the Artists wall or in Search. Their picture, genres, Play and Shuffle are on top; See all on Overview opens Discography. Who they are is the act your albums are by: its MusicBrainz ID from your files or matches, else the act MusicBrainz lists for your album titles, never just the first of the name. Facts come from MusicBrainz, Wikidata and Wikipedia, pictures from FanArt.tv (with a key), Wikidata or Deezer, similar artists and albums from Last.fm (with a key) or Deezer; the page has no links out to the web. A member's page shows their bands' albums from their years in them; a tap on a member, a band or a similar artist opens theirs. Tap a genre for its albums.

👥Album credits

Under an album's tracks: who played, sang, wrote and made it, each with their parts and the tracks they are on, from the files' tags and MusicBrainz.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to set up: the server looks albums up in the background, a second at a time. Eight show at first; More for the rest. Tap a name for their artist page.

🎸Genres on pages

An album's genres as small chips under its cover, and an artist's, the most used across their albums, in English everywhere. A tap opens that genre's albums.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to set up: they come from your files' genre tags. A genre written two ways (Indie Rock, indie rock) is one, spelled as most of your albums spell it; a Qobuz album's French genre (Électronique) shows in English (Electronic). Your files are not changed.

✏️Edit album

Correct an album's title, artist or year and choose its cover. Edits are kept in the server's database; your music files are not changed.

How to set it up and use it. On an album, tap ⋯ → Edit album.

🗂️Music folders

Any folders the server can see, added and removed in the app. Changes show by themselves: an album as it is copied in, a network share's within half a minute.

How to set it up and use it. Open Settings → Music Folders and add a folder from the list of drives and folders the server can see. Changes are read about five seconds after they stop, and at least every 30 seconds while a copy goes on; a network share, a FUSE folder (mergerfs, rclone) or one past the system's limit is looked at every 30 seconds instead. A drive mounted inside a music folder after the server starts (a slow USB disk at boot) is read as it appears; one not mounted keeps its tracks and covers until it is back. A full scan runs every 6 hours (SCAN_INTERVAL_HOURS); ☰ → Rescan library runs one now, then brings Qobuz and Tidal up to date, in that order, where you are signed in with the import on.

💽One album per folder

Each folder is one album. Disc folders inside it, such as Disc 1 or CD2, make one album shown disc by disc, with a two-disc symbol on its cover.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to set up.

📝Untagged files named from folders

A file with no tags takes its artist, album, year, title and track number from its folder and file names. Nothing is written to your files.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to set up. Those albums are then looked up by the Library Scanner like any other.

🏷️Every tag kept

All of each file's tags are read into the server's database, including MusicBrainz IDs, barcode, catalogue number, ISRC and ReplayGain, and used to identify albums.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to set up: tags are read with every scan.

🔎Library Scanner

Names albums from their files' identifiers first, then from MusicBrainz by tracks and lengths, with Apple's iTunes catalogue as a second source. Measures ReplayGain for files without it.

How to set it up and use it. Open Settings → Library Scanner. Clean up there removes albums whose files are gone, after showing counts. Matches of 85% or better are applied (with Undo); the rest are left for you to match; a copy that is every track of a bigger pressing is taken as that record. Your files are not changed. Ask iTunes too and Measure ReplayGain are on the same page; Scheduling sets the hours both run, identification first, then ReplayGain album by album.

📦MusicBrainz pack

An optional download of every MusicBrainz release with a barcode, about 2 GB on disk. Albums with a barcode are matched from it, also while musicbrainz.org is down.

How to set it up and use it. Open Settings → Library Scanner → MusicBrainz pack and tap Download. Folder chooses where it's kept: any folder the server can write to.

▶️Now playing

Cover, track, transport, volume, the queue and history, and a badge saying what the device is being sent. An optional waveform seek bar is drawn from the audio.

How to set it up and use it. Tap the bar at the bottom of the screen. The waveform is off until you switch on Settings → Audio Devices → Waveform. On a large screen, Now playing can shrink to a card you drag around. On the Queue tab, hold a track to select it and more; ⋯ plays the selection now or next, or removes it; Clear all empties the queue.

↪️Move to another device

Pick another room, renderer or phone while music plays and it moves there: the queue goes with it, from the same track and second.

How to set it up and use it. Tap the speaker button (on the bar or Now playing) and choose the device to move to.

📻Random Album Radio

When a device's queue is running out, a random album goes on the end: never one the radio played in the last six months. Switched on per device.

How to set it up and use it. Open Settings → Audio Devices, tap the device and switch on Random album radio. ⋯ → Radio on an album plays it and switches radio on for that device.

📮Last.fm scrobbling

Everything played, in every room, on a Mandarin Bridge or the phone, is scrobbled to your Last.fm account and shown there as now playing.

How to set it up and use it. Get a free key at last.fm/api/account/create and save it with its shared secret in Settings → Setup → API Keys, then tap Connect to Last.fm and allow Mandarin on Last.fm's page. A track is scrobbled at Last.fm's own point (half way or four minutes in); plays made offline or while Last.fm is down are sent when it is back. Disconnect stops it.

🔈Sonos

Play, queue, play next, shuffle, repeat and volume for each Sonos room, and grouping and ungrouping rooms. Sonos plays up to 24-bit / 48 kHz; Mandarin converts anything above.

How to set it up and use it. Sonos rooms are found on the network by themselves (SONOS_HOSTS if not). Pick one with the speaker button; Group zones… in the same list groups them.

📡UPnP/DLNA renderers

WiiM, Chord Poly, streamers, AV receivers and TVs on your network, played like a Sonos room, gapless; one with MPD beside it can be handed the whole queue.

How to set it up and use it. A renderer found on the network is off until you switch it on in Settings → Audio Devices. Then pick it with the speaker button. Look again searches the network now. Where MPD is found beside it (a Chord Poly), Output → Player → Its own player hands MPD the queue, and Output → Gapless → Albums as one stream sends an album as one stream however the device joins files.

🎶Squeezelite players

Squeezelite players and Squeezebox-compatible hardware play from Mandarin over SlimProto, one track on into the next from their own buffer, with Output's upsampling and seeking.

How to set it up and use it. Start Squeezelite on the network: it finds Mandarin by itself on port 3483, or point it at it (squeezelite -s <server-ip>). It shows in Settings → Audio Devices under its own name; switch it on. To pause at a change of rate, start it with a rate list and a delay (-r 44100-768000:500).

🖥️Sound devices on the server

A USB DAC plugged into the server, played like a Sonos room by Mandarin Bridge for Linux beside it.

How to set it up and use it. Install Mandarin Bridge for Linux beside the server: it plays the DAC as a room of its own. Details under Sound devices on the server.

🎚️Audio Devices

Every room, renderer and phone with the rates, depths and formats it takes, your own name for it, and a switch. Renderers play Original or upsampled ×2, ×4 or Max.

How to set it up and use it. Open Settings → Audio Devices and tap a device. The name you give it is what the speaker list and Now playing show. Output is on a renderer's page. Hide / unhide takes devices you don't use off the list; tap it again and tick one under Hidden to bring it back.

💎DSD

A device that takes DSD is sent the DSF or DFF itself, natively or as DoP; PCM can be made DSD64–256 with a choice of three modulators.

How to set it up and use it. On a device's page in Settings → Audio Devices, switch the DSD rates it takes on or off; Output → PCM as DSD and Modulator make PCM DSD. On Linux, a Mandarin Bridge's own page says when the computer's kernel holds back a DAC's native DSD, and the line that frees it.

🔊Volume Levelling

ReplayGain per device: Off, Track, Album or Auto, a target from −14 to −25 LUFS, and a level for tracks with no loudness information.

How to set it up and use it. Open Settings → Audio Devices, tap the device, and set Volume levelling (above DSP). Auto plays albums at album level and mixed tracks at track level.

🎛️DSP

A parametric EQ of up to ten bands per renderer or phone, headphone profiles from AutoEq, and room correction from an impulse response (a WAV from REW or Dirac).

How to set it up and use it. Open Settings → Audio Devices, tap a renderer or the phone, and switch on DSP. Room correction takes one WAV, one for each rate, or a zip of them. Sonos rooms have no DSP here.

⚙️Playback first

Playback keeps a processor core of its own, two while DSP is in use. Scanning, ReplayGain and downloads run on the other cores, at the lowest priority.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to set up on Linux. Settings → Library Scanner → Processor shows the split. PLAYBACK_CORES sets how many cores playback keeps; 0 shares them all.

⭐Smart Picks

Five albums a day from your own library, by artists Deezer lists as related to the ones you've played, topped up from your least-played albums.

How to set it up and use it. ☰ → Smart Picks, or its Home row. Settings → Setup → Smart Picks switches it on or off and sets the hour each day's picks are made.

📰Pitchfork

Pitchfork's latest album reviews and Best New Music, read in the app, with Play on any album you own.

How to set it up and use it. Open ☰ → Pitchfork.

🏢Record labels

Labels from your files' tags or a folder level: a Labels screen, logos from Discogs and FanArt.tv, lookups for albums without a label, and merging of duplicate names.

How to set it up and use it. Switch it on in Settings → Setup → Record labels. Logos need a Discogs token or FanArt.tv key (Settings → Setup → API Keys). On ☰ → Labels, search and #–Z are in the top bar (on a phone or tablet, #–Z at the top right and Search in the row); hold a tile to select labels to merge.

📆Label of the week

With Record labels on, one label from your library on Home all week, with its albums. A new one each Monday.

How to set it up and use it. Switch on Settings → Setup → Record labels; the row then shows on Home.

❤️Favourites

A heart on every album. Hearted albums gather on a Home row.

How to set it up and use it. Tap the heart on an album's page. Tap the Favourites row's title for all of them.

🕒Listen later

Albums put aside to play another time, on a Home row and a screen of their own. Each comes off by itself once every track has been played.

How to set it up and use it. On an album, ⋯ → Listen later, or select several on a wall. Open the list from ☰ → Listen later.

🖼️Share card

The album as a picture: its cover, release, title and artist, its review with whose words they are, Pitchfork's score where there is one, and the duck in its corner.

How to set it up and use it. Tap the share button on an album's page or Now playing; there is nothing to set up. In the Android app, Copy image puts the card on the clipboard, to paste into a forum or a message, and Download saves it to Pictures/Mandarin; in a browser, Share… or Download.

📺Wall display

A full-screen now playing page for a TV or tablet; on a big TV its reviews and covers are drawn large enough to read from across the room.

How to set it up and use it. Open http://<server-ip>:3500/display on the screen. Its switch (on by default) and options are in Settings → Setup → Wall Display; off, it is off entirely. Tap the screen for its controls; Shelf › opens Shelf.

📱The Android app

Mandarin's interface drawn natively by the app, which finds the server home or away and asks again when it's back; lock-screen controls, volume keys, a widget, Android Auto, its updates.

How to set it up and use it. Download it from Downloads and install it (Android 8.0 or newer). Enter the port; it finds the server on your Wi-Fi.

🎧This phone

The phone is a player of its own: speaker, headphones or Bluetooth, with queue, history and radio. At home, your other devices can play to it while the app runs.

How to set it up and use it. In the Android app, pick This phone with the speaker button.

🔌USB DAC on the phone

The app's own USB Audio driver plays to a DAC on the phone's port at the file's rate and depth, without Android's mixer; DSD natively or as DoP.

How to set it up and use it. Plug the DAC in. It shows on This phone's page in Settings → Audio Devices; switch on USB direct there. Pull it out while playing and the phone pauses, as when headphones come out.

🌉Mandarin Bridge

A USB DAC on an Android device, a Linux computer or a Raspberry Pi, as a room Mandarin plays to: over SlimProto, a continuous stream of PCM.

How to set it up and use it. Install it from Downloads: the Android app, the Linux install line, or the Raspberry Pi image to flash. It finds Mandarin by itself; plug the DAC in, then switch the Bridge on in Settings → Audio Devices. Details under Mandarin Bridge.

📈Upsampling and output

For each streamer or DAC: Bit-perfect or upsampled recipes, filters in either phase, shaped dither, PCM made DSD, Headroom against clipping, and a safety net when the server falls behind.

How to set it up and use it. Open Settings → Audio Devices, tap a streamer or DAC, and choose a recipe under Sound, or open Advanced for each row: Mode, a filter for CD-rate and one for hi-res sources, Phase, Highest rate, Bit depth, the DAC's real bits and Dither, DSD as PCM, PCM as DSD and its Modulator, Headroom when upsampling (Auto), Gapless. All made as each track plays; an Output the server can't make in time, or the device can't play, is put back to the last that played well, and the device's page says so. Details under Upsampling and output.

🛤️Signal path

Under the album on Now playing, one short line of what goes out (Gaussian steep ×16 · 7th-order · DSD256). A tap shows each step to the DAC.

How to set it up and use it. Play to a room and look under the album on Now playing; tap it for every step: Source, Bit depth conversion, Volume levelling where it is on, Headroom, Output with how fast it is made (Keeping up at 4.2× real time), and the device's own word; the line turns red where samples were clipped. The ✓ shows only when the device itself says the samples reach the DAC untouched. Details under Signal path.

📊SQ-tool

Proves a zone's DAC gets exactly what is in the file, sample by sample: Mandarin plays a song, records what the DAC is fed, and SQ-tool compares them.

How to set it up and use it. Open Settings → SQ-tool, choose a song and a zone (a Mandarin Bridge on Android), and press Record. Details under SQ-tool.

⬇️Downloads

Albums kept on the phone as the original files or Opus 256, played without the server. Smart Picks, the Album of the day and new albums can download by themselves.

How to set it up and use it. On an album, ⋯ → Download to this phone. Quality, the download folder, Wi-Fi only and automatic downloads are in Settings → Setup → Downloads in the app.

📂Music on this phone

A folder of music already on the phone, watched for changes, on Home with Focus and Sort and on artists' pages, played like the library. The server never sees it.

How to set it up and use it. In the app, Settings → Music Folders → On this phone and choose the folder. Selected with other albums and played on This phone, they go into the one queue.

✈️Offline mode

One switch: only the music on the phone shows, and the server isn't used. With no connection the app does the same by itself, and reconnects when it can.

How to set it up and use it. In the Android app, ☰ → Offline mode. While offline, a small Offline pill sits at the top of the screen: tap it to see why (Offline mode, or the server can't be reached).

🌍Away from home

On mobile data the app reaches the server through its own Tailscale, by itself, with no ports opened. Away, only the phone plays: Opus 256 or original files.

How to set it up and use it. Settings → Setup → Away from home on the server: Sign in to Tailscale. Then in the app, the same page: This phone. Open the app once at home. Details under Away from home.

📀Mandarin OSNew

The recommended way to run Mandarin: a lightweight operating system made only for it, on a PC of its own, with its own system page to watch over everything.

How to set it up and use it. Write its image to a USB stick, start the PC from it and install; details under Mandarin OS. Mandarin is then at http://<box address>:3500 and the system page at http://<box address>/: storage and USB drives, the network, Mandarin Bridge, the logs, restart and shut down. It updates itself in place, and goes back by itself to the version that worked if a new one doesn't start.

🎨UI Settings

Text sizes, grid layout (Auto, 3 or 2 columns, or List) and tile size, saved on each device. On a desktop, text goes up to +100%.

How to set it up and use it. Open Settings → Setup → UI Settings and pick from each list; it applies at once.

👤An account, or none

One username and password kept on the server, or none: a TV showing the shelf or the wall needs nothing typed. The password itself is never sent.

How to set it up and use it. On first visit from a device at home, create the account or choose Use without an account. Settings → Account → Ask for a sign-in switches it on or off: off, the home network and your Tailscale network come straight in, anywhere else still signs in. Details under Your account.

⏻Restart and shut down

Restart Mandarin, or shut it down so it stops finding and controlling your speakers until you start it again.

How to set it up and use it. Open the side menu (☰) and tap the power button in its top-right corner, then Restart or Shut down. On Mandarin OS the computer shuts down too; its power button starts it all again.

💾Backup and restore

Back up settings, players, collection, API keys (with the Tailscale identity) and the whole database, at home or away: to the server, or from the app to a file.

How to set it up and use it. Open Settings → Backup & restore, tick what to include, then Back up to the server (or, in the app, Back up to a file on this phone). To restore, pick a backup (or the file), tick the parts and tap Restore. Mandarin keeps a backup of how things were first, then restarts. What a backup holds. The API keys part (and the whole database) holds your Discogs, FanArt.tv and Last.fm keys and the Last.fm sign-in, the Qobuz and Tidal sign-ins (tokens, never a password) and the server's Tailscale identity. Without a passphrase they are in the file as they are, so keep the file as you would keep them. Type a passphrase (8 characters or more) before backing up and they are sealed (AES-256-GCM; v0.9.0): restoring them asks for it, and nothing opens them without it. Everything else in a sealed backup restores without it. A backup never holds your password or other devices' sign-ins.

🎼Qobuz

Sign in with your Qobuz subscription: your favourites and purchases become albums here, and the Qobuz browser plays anything in the catalogue, streamed through the server.

How to set it up and use it. Settings → Services → Qobuz: sign in. Then ☰ → Qobuz to search and play; + on a cover adds an album to your Qobuz favourites (a ✓ once added) and so to the library, as does ⋯ → Add to Qobuz favourites on its page. Details under Qobuz.

🎵Tidal

Sign in with your Tidal subscription on tidal.com: your favourite albums become albums here, and the Tidal browser plays anything in the catalogue, streamed through the server.

How to set it up and use it. Settings → Services → Tidal → Sign in on tidal.com: open the link, sign in there, come back. Then ☰ → Tidal to search and play; + on a cover adds an album to your Tidal favourites (a ✓ once added) and so to the library, as does ⋯ → Add to Tidal favourites on its page. Details under Tidal.

📝LoggingNew

Always on: the server, every Mandarin Bridge, the Android app and the page log what happened, without personal data, kept on the server for you to send to the developer.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to switch on. In a browser, Settings → Setup → Logs → Download all logs: every log as one text file, in time order. In the Android app, Settings → Setup → Logging → Save log (to Downloads/Mandarin, the server's and the Mandarin Bridges' logs with it); Clear log empties it. On Mandarin OS, the system page's Support → Download all logs. Look through it before you send it. Details under Logs.

🔄Updates

The server updates itself from GitHub and checks every 12 hours. The Android app and every Mandarin Bridge have their own Check for update beside the server's.

How to set it up and use it. Settings → Updates → Check for update beside the server, the Android app (in the app) or a Bridge; once it says Update available, tap it again. The server starts again in a few seconds, the app's update goes to Android's installer, a Linux or Raspberry Pi Bridge updates itself, and an Android Bridge shows a notification whose tap downloads the update in its browser. The server and the Bridges install only an update signed with Mandarin's release key.
