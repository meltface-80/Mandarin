Away from home

Leave the house and the Android app keeps working over mobile data. No ports are opened on your router: the phone reaches the server over Tailscale, a private network between your own devices.

Set up once — Tailscale is built in

  1. On the server, open Settings → Setup → Away from home and tap Sign in to Tailscale. Sign in on Tailscale's page (a free account is enough). The server joins your tailnet by itself as musicd.
  2. On the phone, sign Mandarin's app in to the same account: Settings → Setup → Away from home → This phone. The app carries its own Tailscale too — no Tailscale app needed.
  3. Open Mandarin once at home: the app learns the server's tailnet address.

From then on the app follows the phone's network: on your Wi-Fi it uses the server's home address; on mobile data it goes over its own Tailscale, and the page, the queue and the music carry on without a reload.

Anything else signed in to your tailnet — an iPhone with the Tailscale app, a laptop — opens the address Away from home shows.

Use Tailscale, not a port forward

Mandarin speaks plain http, as a server at home has no certificate: safe at home, and over Tailscale, whose own link is encrypted. A port forwarded on your router is neither — what the app sends, its sign-in included, would cross the internet unencrypted. The server's log says so whenever a plain-http request arrives from outside your home network.

Away, only the phone plays

Whatever reaches the server from outside your home network — Tailscale included — is offered one room: the phone asking, as This phone. The Sonos rooms aren't listed, and nothing away can play to them, pause, group or mute them, or reach another phone. The server decides this by where each request comes from, so it holds for any device: an iPhone or a laptop on Tailscale can browse the library but has nothing to play to. At home everything is as before.

Away, tracks stream as Opus 256 kbps by default (about a quarter of a CD-quality FLAC's data), made through a 64-bit float resample to Opus's 48 kHz and decoded on the phone by the app's own libopus; Settings → Setup → Downloads → Stream as in the app can send the original files instead. Albums you've downloaded play from the phone. On a weak signal the track that's playing comes first: the tracks after it are fetched only once it has enough.

At home the app keeps its own Tailscale running only while the phone is playing, so walking out of the house doesn't stop the music; back home, the phone plays from home again, and the engine stops as soon as the app is off screen or the phone stops playing. The engine also stops itself when nothing goes through it (two and a half minutes, with the app neither on screen nor playing) or when it is busy for nothing (a fifth of a core for a minute, with nothing passing through), so Android has no cause to end the app for it. It starts again in seconds when the app is used or you leave, its sign-in kept on the phone.

For a server with no one at it, give it an auth key instead, as the server's TS_AUTHKEY (see Settings for the server). Don't advertise your home subnet from the server's Tailscale: requests through a subnet router arrive from a home address, and the server can't tell they're away.

The server's Tailscale keeps its identity in the data folder, so an update or a reinstall is the same machine. TAILSCALE=off leaves it out; TS_HOSTNAME names it something other than musicd. Tailscale installed on the host still works as before (the server uses its own first when both are there, or TAILSCALE_ADDRESS to say which).
