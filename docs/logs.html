Logs

Mandarin keeps one log of everything: the server, Mandarin OS, every Mandarin Bridge, the Android app and the page in a browser, a line each in time order, in one plain-text file. There is nothing to switch on, and it can't be switched off, so a fault seen once is already in it.

Each line says when (in UTC), how bad (INFO, WARN or ERROR) and where it came from: [server], [os], [bridge and its name], [app and the phone's name], [web and the browser's name]. Nothing in it is sent anywhere unless you send it.

2026-11-15 09:43:12.345 UTC  INFO   [server] [scan] 12 albums added
2026-11-15 09:43:13.002 UTC  INFO   [os] [usb] mounted /data/Storage/USB/Music SSD (exfat)
2026-11-15 09:43:14.120 UTC  WARN   [bridge Kitchen] [player] the DAC went away
2026-11-15 09:43:15.871 UTC  INFO   [app Pixel 8] [NET] server GET /api/zones → ok in 40 ms

Where it is, and sending it

  • On Mandarin OS: the shared folder's Logs (smb://<box address>/Data/Logs), read-only: Mandarin_log.txt is the newest. Copy it from there and send it.
  • The app, the page and the system page have no log of their own to show or save, and the app keeps none on the phone: its lines reach the server within seconds.

The log is made without personal data (below). Even so, please look through it before you send it: what you send is your choice.

Captured

What it captures

  • Screens: each screen opened and how long after the tap, what is on screen, where each tap fell, and anything that doesn't flow: a screen shown for a moment between two others, one opened and closed by itself, a frame that stalls.
  • Requests: each request the app makes to the server: its address on the server, how long it took and whether it worked. The values in it are left out.
  • The way to the server: home, away (Tailscale) or offline, and networks coming and going.
  • The phone's player: playing or paused, the track's number in the queue, its position and the volume.
  • The app: its memory, crashes and freezes, and Android's own lines for Mandarin.
  • The app's Tailscale: whether it is running and the CPU time it has used, and when it is stopped, why, with its last lines (addresses taken out).
  • The Mandarin Bridges: every Bridge Mandarin knows, under its name: what it logs (the player, the DAC, a recording, an update), fetched by the server every minute; on Mandarin OS, the box's own Bridge straight from the system.
  • The page: what goes wrong in a browser's page (an error nothing caught), under the browser's name.
  • Mandarin OS: its scripts and services, the system page, its updates, USB drives, health checks, Samba, and the system's word on each of them.
  • The server: each line it logs, with what is yours alone taken out: what it is as it starts, how it is every five minutes (memory, threads, free space), and an error with its stack.
Left out

What it leaves out

  • Passwords, keys and tokens.
  • Email addresses, account names and logins.
  • Internet and Tailscale addresses.
  • Your own home folder's name in a path.

It keeps what helps find a fault: home-network addresses, the names of rooms and devices, what was playing, error messages.

How it is kept

One file, Mandarin_log.txt. At 8 MB it becomes Mandarin_log.01.txt (the one before .02, and so on) and a new one is begun: ten files at most, the oldest let go, so it never grows without end. On Mandarin OS every part says its lines to the system's journal and one small service alone writes the file, so a crash of Mandarin loses none of its lines. The app keeps nothing on the phone: its lines wait in memory and reach the server within seconds, or as soon as it can be reached again.
