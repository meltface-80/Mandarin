Mandarin

Your own music files, played to Sonos rooms, UPnP renderers and your phone

A small server on a machine you own scans your music folders. Every Sonos room in the house, any UPnP/DLNA renderer you switch on — a WiiM, a Chord Poly, a streamer, an AV receiver, a TV — and the Mandarin app play from it, controlled from a browser, an iPhone home screen, or the Android app, designed for this server. No subscriptions, no streaming accounts. Definitely no nonsense about better sound quality: it plays the files on your server.

v0.6.6 Docker MIT Sonos UPnP / DLNA Android app USB DAC

How it works

Mandarin is a server and the app built for it. The server scans your library, drives Sonos rooms with the control ported from the Caldera Sonos Bridge, drives UPnP/DLNA renderers — a WiiM, a Chord Poly, a streamer, an AV receiver, a TV, anything with AVTransport — through their own transport, gapless, with each one's sample rates read from the device, and plays to the Android app on the phone itself or through a USB DAC on its port. It plays your files without ridiculous claims.

Tracks go into the speaker's own Sonos queue, so playback is gapless and a skip is instant; a UPnP renderer is handed each next track ahead of time for the same reason. Each device fetches the audio straight from the server — as the file itself when it can play it, converted to what it takes when it can't.

🏠Home, the way Mandarin does it

Random Album and the Album of the day under the greeting, then Not played in six months, Listen later, Favourites, Recently played, Smart Picks, Label of the week, random albums and genres — rows you reorder or switch off.

📅Album of the day

The same album on every device, chosen by the server at 00:01. Once played, from anywhere, it's gone until the next 00:01.

🔍Instant search & focus

Search albums, artists and labels as you type. Sort and focus the library by genre, decade, format, sample rate, bit depth, label or date added.

💿Album pages

Tracks, year and label, favourite, listen later, download and share. Previous and next, or a swipe, step through the albums of the row you came from.

🔖Listen later

Albums put aside to hear, on a Home row and a wall of their own. Each comes off by itself once every track has played.

📅Label of the week

With Record labels on, one label from your library on Home all week, with its albums — a new one each Monday.

❤Favourites

A heart on any album, gathered on a Home row of their own.

✨Smart Picks & Discover

Five records a day from your own library, by acts near the ones you play, and new releases by the artists you listen to.

📰Write-ups & reviews

Album and artist stories from Wikipedia and Qobuz's editorial pages, and the Pitchfork score with a link to the review — no account needed.

📖Pitchfork

The latest reviews and Best New Music to read in the app, with Play on any record you own.

〰Waveform

The seek bar draws the shape of the track, measured from your file and kept, so it is instant after the first play.

📤Share card

The record as an image, where to hear it and where to read about it, and three acts worth hearing next — the ones you own play straight away.

📻Random Album Radio

When a player's queue runs out, another whole album you haven't played lately goes on. Per player.

🔊Rooms

Volume per speaker, shuffle and repeat, and move what's playing to another room or player. Sonos rooms can be grouped here too, and Sonos keeps them in step itself.

📺Wall display

A full-screen now-playing page for a TV or tablet at /display, with reviews, artist stories and more from your library.

🎶Playlists

Playlists you build, Dynamic Playlists that follow a saved focus, and sharing and importing as a playlist file you can pass on.

✎Edit album

Correct the title, artist or year and find a cover. Edits live in the database; your files stay exactly as they are.

📁Music folders

Any number of folders the server can see, added in Settings and watched, so new and deleted albums show up by themselves.

🎯Library Scanner

Albums identified by their files' identifiers first — MusicBrainz ID, barcode, catalogue number, ISRC — then MusicBrainz by tracks and lengths, iTunes as a second opinion. ReplayGain measured for untagged files.

🔈Volume Levelling

ReplayGain set per device: Track, Album or Auto, a target from −14 to −25 LUFS, peaks respected. Sonos, renderers and the phone, downloads included.

🎧UPnP/DLNA renderers

A WiiM, a Chord Poly, a streamer, an AV receiver, a TV: found on the network, switched on when you say, gapless, with volume, radio and everything a room has. DSD sent as DSD where taken.

🎚Audio Devices

Every player with what it takes — rates, depths, formats — a name of your own, and Original or Upsample ×2, ×4 or Max output per renderer.

🎼DSP

A parametric EQ of up to ten bands per renderer or phone, the curve drawn as you edit, and headphone profiles from AutoEq or a pasted ParametricEQ.txt.

🏷Record labels

From the files' tags or a folder level: a Labels screen, logos from Discogs and FanArt.tv, lookups for untagged albums, and merging of duplicates.

📱This phone

In the Android app the phone is a room of its own — speaker, headphones, Bluetooth — with the queue, history and radio like any other. At home your other devices can play to it too.

🔌USB DAC

The app's own USB Audio driver feeds a DAC on the phone's port at the file's rate and depth, DSD natively or as DoP, Android's mixer out of the way. Volume starts low, with a limit, or fixed.

⬇Downloads

Albums kept on the phone, in a folder you choose, as the files themselves or as Opus 256, played with no server at all; today's picks kept automatically if you like.

🎵Music on this phone

A folder of files already on the phone, watched for changes, shown and played like the library.

✈️Offline mode

One switch in the menu: only the music on the phone shows, and the server isn't used. With no connection the app does the same by itself, and reconnects on its own.

📍Away from home

On mobile data the app reaches the server over its built-in Tailscale, no ports opened. Tracks come as Opus 256; only the phone plays.

🔒One account

A username and password kept on the server, SRP sign-in, every device listed in Settings. The server and the app update themselves.

New since the last beta

Everything added and improved from v0.5.50 to v0.6.6.

MusicBrainz pack

Every MusicBrainz release with a barcode, kept on the server and refreshed weekly; barcodes match without asking musicbrainz.org. Optional: an 845 MB download, kept in a folder you choose.

UI Settings

Album and artist text, grid screen titles, menu and Home text, grid layout (Auto, 3 or 2 columns, or List) and tile size, set on each device.

Find a label

Search from the Labels screen's top bar, and turn the order round, # to Z or Z to #.

Library controls in the top bar

Focus, Sort and search sit in the Library screen's top bar; the search opens over them, and × clears, then closes.

Titles beside Back

An artist's album count and name, and a label's name, sit beside Back at the top of the screen.

Settings on a tablet or desktop

Settings opens beside the page at the side menu's width, and each settings page only as wide as it needs.

Multi-disc albums

A two-disc symbol on the cover in every grid for an album of several discs, from disc folders or disc numbers.

Random Album

The first tile under Home's greeting, a brass disc turning in its middle: one tap plays a whole album you haven't heard lately.

Album of the day, kept by the server

One album for every device, chosen by the server at 00:01 and remembered through updates and restarts; once played, from anywhere, it's gone until the next 00:01.

Not played in six months

Hidden until Mandarin has six months of your listening, then offers the albums you haven't heard since.

Now playing goes back

Opened over an album, its corner button returns to that album. On large screens it can shrink to a card and be dragged.

API keys checked

The Discogs and FanArt.tv boxes show a ✓ when the service accepts the key, or ✕ when it refuses it.

Untagged albums named from folders

A file with no tags takes its artist, album, year, title and track from its folder and file names, then is identified on MusicBrainz; nothing is written to the files.

One album per folder

Two copies of a record in separate folders are two albums; disc folders such as Disc 1 or CD2 inside an album's folder are one album, shown disc by disc.

Every tag kept

All of each file's tags and identifiers — MusicBrainz IDs, barcode, catalogue number, ISRC, ReplayGain — read into the server's database.

Library Scanner

Albums identified by what their files carry first, then by MusicBrainz and iTunes; ReplayGain measured for files without it. Under Music Folders in Settings.

Volume Levelling

ReplayGain set per device — Off, Track, Album or Auto — with a target from −14 to −25 LUFS and a level for tracks of unknown loudness.

DSD to renderers

A renderer that takes DSD files, such as a Chord Poly, is sent the DSF or DFF itself, at the DSD rates switched on for it.

Move to another device

Moving what's playing to another room, renderer or phone carries the queue over and plays on from the same track and second.

Play to another phone

A phone running the app is a player for your other devices at home, under its own name, while the app runs.

Offline mode

One switch: only the music on the phone shows and the server isn't asked. The app also opens with no signal and finds the server again by itself.

Share card saved

In the app, Download saves the card to the phone's Pictures/Mandarin.

Help behind ⓘ

Every setting's longer explanation opens from an ⓘ after its name, with a short line left under it.

Release candidates

The app, its update check and the server's updater read 0.6.0-RC builds in order, so updates arrive one after another.

What each device is sent

Each track goes the way the device can take it, and the Now playing badge says what was sent. Conversion is ffmpeg with the SoX resampler and triangular dither; a converted track plays while it is still being written, the next tracks are prepared ahead, and replays come from a cache.

Sonos

Up to 24-bit / 48 kHz

Within that, the file as stored. Above it, or in a format Sonos can't read, FLAC at 24/48.

UPnP / DLNA renderer

Its own ceiling, read from the device

Within it, the file as stored. Above it, FLAC at the best rate the device takes in the file's family. Upsample ×2, ×4 or Max if you choose, from its page in Audio Devices.

The Android app

The phone's own output

The file as stored within 24/48, FLAC 24/48 above it. Away from home, Opus 256.

USB DAC on the phone

USB direct

The file at its own rate and depth where the DAC takes it, DSD natively or as DoP, FLAC at the DAC's best rate otherwise.

Everything else

Formats nothing plays as they are

APE, WavPack, WMA Lossless, 24-bit WAV, more than two channels: FLAC, in stereo.

Install with Docker

On an always-on Linux machine on the same network as your speakers — a NAS, a Raspberry Pi 4/5 on a 64-bit OS, a home server. Fill in your music folders and time zone and copy the command.

        --network host is required. Speakers are found by multicast, which does not cross
        Docker's default bridge network, and they fetch audio from this machine's own address.
        Docker Desktop for macOS and Windows has no real host networking, so this needs a Linux host.
      

      

        Keep the musicd-server-data volume. It holds the library database, play history,
        playlists, settings and the artwork and conversion caches. The command above is a swap, not a
        fresh start — re-run it to update.
      

      

        More than one music folder? Add each one above. A single folder is mounted as
        /music; with several, each becomes its own folder inside it —
        /mnt/nas/Albums as /music/Albums, /mnt/usb/Vinyl as
        /music/Vinyl — and the scan reads them all as one library. All are
        mounted read-only. Adding or removing a folder later is the same command re-run; albums
        in a folder that is gone drop out at the next scan.

        Android
        
The Mandarin app

        
Bespoke, and designed from the ground up for the Mandarin server. Enter the port and it
           finds the server on your Wi-Fi by itself — or type the address.
           On top of the interface it adds lock-screen and notification controls, volume keys, a

Environment variables

    
All optional; pass any with -e NAME=value. Everything else is in the
       app's Settings and kept in the data volume.

    

      

        
VariableDefaultPurpose

        
PORT3500The interface, the API, and where speakers fetch audio.

        
TZUTCAlbum of the day changes over at 00:01 and Smart Picks at local midnight.

        
SONOS_HOSTS—A speaker's IP, for when multicast discovery is unreliable. One is enough.

        
UPNP_HOSTS—UPnP/DLNA renderers to ask by address (an IP or a description URL), for when multicast discovery misses them.

        
IDENTIFYon0 leaves the album identification scan out entirely.

        
ITUNES_COUNTRYUSThe Apple store the identification scan's iTunes lookups use (GB, DE…).

        
SERVER_IPautoThe address speakers fetch audio from, on hosts with several interfaces.

        
INCLUDE_ZONES—Offer only these rooms, e.g. Kitchen,Study.

        
EXCLUDE_ZONES—Offer every room except these.

TRANSCODE_CONCURRENCY2Tracks converted at once.

Troubleshooting

    
http://<server-ip>:3500/api/health answers without signing in;
       /api/status (signed in) says what the server can see. Forgot the password?
       docker exec musicd-server node reset-password.js, then create the account again.

    

      

        No rooms
        
"No Sonos rooms found yet"

        
Check the container is on host networking and on the same subnet as the speakers, then
           look at sonos.error in /api/status. Setting
           SONOS_HOSTS to one speaker's IP skips discovery.

      

      

        Silence
        
A room skips every track

        
The speakers can't reach the server. On a machine with several network interfaces set
           SERVER_IP to its LAN address, and check nothing blocks the port.

      

      

        No albums
        
The library is empty

        
/api/status shows music_dir and index_count. Check
           the -v …:/music:ro mount and that the folder is readable.

      

      

        Hi-res
        
96 kHz files won't play

        
/api/health must say "ffmpeg": true. The Docker image includes it;
           a hand-run server needs ffmpeg on the PATH.
